## Level 1 — Words & Meaning

**1.1** — *Hamartia*, the New Testament word for sin, literally means:
- **✓ missing the mark**
- breaking a written law
- owing a debt you can't repay
- rebelling against a king

> All four are biblical *pictures* of sin, but *hamartia* is the archer's word —
> falling short of the target (Romans 3:23). Sin is first a failure to hit what
> we were made for, not only rule-breaking.

**1.2** — Which Greek term names the claim that the Son is *of the same being* as
the Father?
- **✓ homoousios**
- hypostasis
- perichōrēsis
- kenōsis

> *Homoousios* = "same being," fixed at Nicaea (325). One iota separates it from
> *homoiousios*, "similar being" — the compromise Nicaea rejected. *Hypostasis*
> is "person," *perichōrēsis* the Persons' mutual indwelling, *kenōsis* Christ's
> self-emptying.

**1.3** — *Theopneustos*, Paul's word for Scripture in 2 Timothy 3:16, means:
- **✓ God-breathed**
- God-spoken
- God-given
- God-approved

> *Theos* + *pneō*, "to breathe." Scripture is breathed out by God — divine in
> origin, even as real human authors wrote it.

**1.4** — *Perichōrēsis*, the term for the inner life of the Trinity, means:
- **✓ mutual indwelling**
- eternal begetting
- undivided essence
- equal in glory

> The three Persons live completely *in* one another (John 17:21) — not three
> parts standing side by side. The other three phrases are true of God but name
> different things.

**1.5** — *Koinōnia*, Paul's word for the Lord's Supper in 1 Corinthians 10:16,
carries the sense of:
- **✓ a sharing / participation in**
- a remembrance of
- a thanksgiving for
- a covenant renewal with

> "The cup of blessing... is it not a *koinōnia* in the blood of Christ?" The
> table is real participation in Christ, more than a memorial. *Anamnēsis* is
> "remembrance," *eucharistia* "thanksgiving" — adjacent words, different weight.

## Level 2 — Scripture & History

**2.1** — Paul writes that if one thing is not true, "your faith is worthless;
you are still in your sins" (1 Corinthians 15:17). He means:
- **✓ the resurrection**
- the incarnation
- the atoning death
- the second coming

> 1 Corinthians 15 stakes the whole gospel on the risen Christ. Take the
> resurrection away and, Paul says, forgiveness itself falls with it.

**2.2** — The word *homoousios* was written into the Nicene Creed (325) to rule
out:
- **✓ Arianism**
- Pelagianism
- Nestorianism
- Gnosticism

> Arius taught the Son was the first and highest *creature*. *Homoousios* — same
> being as the Father — says the Son is fully God and uncreated. (Pelagianism is
> about sin and grace; Nestorianism about Christ's two natures; Gnosticism about
> matter and secret knowledge.)

**2.3** — Augustine developed and defended the doctrine of original sin against:
- **✓ Pelagius**
- Arius
- Nestorius
- Marcion

> Pelagius taught that people can choose good without grace. Augustine held that
> Adam's fall left all humanity needing grace even to *will* the good — the
> backdrop that makes the gospel remarkable.

**2.4** — Chalcedon (451) confessed Christ in "two natures, without confusion,
without change, without division, without separation." It condemned Nestorius,
who split Christ into two persons — and also:
- **✓ Eutyches, who blended the two natures into one**
- Arius, who denied the Son's deity
- the Docetists, who denied Christ had a real body
- Sabellius, who denied the three Persons

> The four "withouts" guard both flanks at once: the natures are neither divided
> into two persons nor fused into a third kind of thing.

**2.5** — "To the one who does not work but believes in Him who justifies the
ungodly, his faith is credited as righteousness" (Romans 4:5) is the anchor verse
for:
- **✓ justification**
- faith
- grace
- repentance

> Paul's sharpest statement that righteousness is *credited*, not earned — the
> verse Luther built the Reformation recovery of justification on.

## Level 3 — Connections

**3.1** — Which doctrine describes the condition that makes *grace* necessary?
- **✓ sin and the fall**
- repentance
- suffering
- discipleship

> Grace is unearned rescue; it only means anything against a fallen, helpless
> condition. "Where sin increased, grace abounded all the more" (Romans 5:20).

**3.2** — Justification declares us righteous. Which doctrine names the new
*relationship* we are brought into?
- **✓ adoption**
- regeneration
- sanctification
- salvation

> Justification is the courtroom — the verdict "not guilty." Adoption is the
> family room — "my child." Romans 8:15: the Spirit of adoption by which we cry
> "Abba, Father."

**3.3** — A believer says, "I was justified the day I trusted Christ, and I've
been sanctified a little more every day since." What distinction is she making
correctly?
- **✓ a finished verdict vs. an ongoing work**
- a legal change vs. a relational one
- God's part vs. our part
- the Father's work vs. the Spirit's

> Justification is once-for-all and complete; sanctification is lifelong and
> gradual. Both are grace. Collapsing the two produces either pride or despair.

**3.4** — Regeneration and adoption both happen at conversion, but they answer
different questions. Regeneration answers "what changed inside me?" Adoption
answers:
- **✓ "whose family am I in?"**
- "am I really forgiven?"
- "will I make it to the end?"
- "what do I have to do now?"

> Regeneration = a new nature, born of the Spirit (Titus 3:5). Adoption = a new
> status, a permanent place in God's household.

**3.5** — Which doctrine corrects the idea that God's endgame is to evacuate the
saved off to heaven forever?
- **✓ new heaven and new earth**
- the second coming
- eternal life
- final judgment

> Revelation 21: God *renews* creation and comes down to dwell with His people.
> The Christian hope is resurrection and a restored earth, not escape from it.
