## L1 — Words & Meaning (25)

### Trinity & Nature of God

**d1-l1-homoousios** · card 1
Which Greek term names the claim that the Son is *of the same being* as the Father?
- **✓ homoousios**
- hypostasis
- perichōrēsis
- kenōsis

> *Homoousios* = "same being," fixed at Nicaea (325). One iota separates it from
> *homoiousios*, "similar being" — the compromise Nicaea rejected. *Hypostasis*
> is "person," *perichōrēsis* the Persons' mutual indwelling, *kenōsis* Christ's
> self-emptying.

**d1-l1-perichoresis** · card 5
*Perichōrēsis*, the term for the inner life of the Trinity, means:
- **✓ mutual indwelling**
- eternal begetting
- undivided essence
- equal in glory

> The three Persons live completely *in* one another (John 17:21) — not three
> parts standing side by side. The other three phrases are true of God but name
> different things.

**d1-l1-bara** · card 6
In Genesis 1:1 the Hebrew verb *bara* ("created") is unlike ordinary words for
making because:
- **✓ in the Old Testament it is used only with God as its subject**
- it always refers to shaping something from material already there
- it is interchangeable with the ordinary Hebrew word for "make"
- it means "to arrange" rather than "to originate"

> *Bara* is never used of human making and never requires raw material — it
> carries the sense of bringing into being what was not there. *Yatsar*, "to
> form," is the word for shaping existing stuff.

**d1-l1-pantokrator** · card 8
*Pantokratōr*, the title behind "God's sovereignty," is best translated:
- **✓ ruler of all / holder of all power**
- maker of all
- knower of all
- judge of all

> *Pan* ("all") + *kratōr* ("ruler"). It names God's actual rule over everything,
> not merely His ability to rule. The other three are true of God but are
> different words — Creator, omniscient, Judge.

### Jesus Christ

**d1-l1-hypostatic-union** · card 11
The term *hypostatic union* (*henōsis hypostatikē*) refers to:
- **✓ two natures, divine and human, united in Christ's one person**
- the union of the three persons in one divine essence
- the believer's union with Christ by faith
- the joining of Christ's will to the Father's will

> *Hypostasis* = "person." The hypostatic union joins full deity and full
> humanity in the single person of the Son — not a Trinity term, and not the
> same as the believer's union with Christ.

**d1-l1-kippur** · card 13
The Hebrew word *kippur*, behind "atonement," carries the root idea of:
- **✓ covering**
- cutting / making a covenant
- ransoming a slave
- washing clean

> *Kippur* — to cover. On the Day of Atonement the blood "covered" the people's
> sin before God. "Ransom" and "cleansing" are other biblical pictures of the
> same rescue, but different words.

**d1-l1-anastasis** · card 14
*Anastasis*, the New Testament word for resurrection, literally means:
- **✓ a standing up again**
- a waking from sleep
- a passing over
- a carrying up

> *Ana* ("again/up") + *stasis* ("standing") — the body raised and standing, not
> a metaphor for the soul surviving death. "Carrying up" (*analēpsis*) is the
> word for the *ascension*.

### Holy Spirit

**d1-l1-parakletos** · card 16
*Paraklētos*, Jesus' name for the Spirit in John 14, means:
- **✓ one called alongside to help**
- one sent ahead to prepare the way
- one who speaks for God to the people
- one who covers over faults

> *Para* ("alongside") + *klētos* ("called") — an advocate or helper summoned to
> your side. "One who speaks for God to the people" is a prophet (*prophētēs*).

**d1-l1-palingenesia** · card 17
*Palingenesia*, translated "regeneration," means:
- **✓ new birth / becoming again**
- turning around
- growing up to maturity
- being set apart

> *Palin* ("again") + *genesis* ("birth") — the once-for-all new birth by the
> Spirit (Titus 3:5). "Turning around" is repentance (*metanoia*); "being set
> apart" is sanctification (*hagiasmos*).

**d1-l1-charismata** · card 20
The word *charismata* ("spiritual gifts") is formed directly from the Greek word
for:
- **✓ grace (*charis*)**
- power (*dunamis*)
- service (*diakonia*)
- calling (*klēsis*)

> A *charisma* is a "grace-gift" — a concrete expression of God's grace given for
> the good of others (1 Cor 12:7), not a reward for merit or a badge of status.

### Salvation & Gospel

**d1-l1-hamartia** · card 22
*Hamartia*, the New Testament word for sin, literally means:
- **✓ missing the mark**
- breaking a written law
- owing a debt you can't repay
- rebelling against a king

> All four are biblical *pictures* of sin, but *hamartia* is the archer's word —
> falling short of the target (Romans 3:23). Sin is first a failure to hit what
> we were made for, not only rule-breaking.

**d1-l1-charis** · card 24
In Paul's usage, *charis* ("grace") most precisely means:
- **✓ undeserved favor, freely given**
- a reward earned by faith
- a second chance after failure
- pity shown to the weak

> *Charis* is a gift with no strings and no prior claim on it (Eph 2:8–9).
> Making it a reward-for-faith or a second chance smuggles merit back in.

**d1-l1-metanoia** · card 26
*Metanoia*, the word translated "repentance," means:
- **✓ a change of mind that changes direction**
- deep sorrow over sin
- confession of specific sins
- making amends for wrongs done

> *Meta* ("change") + *nous* ("mind"). Sorrow, confession, and restitution may
> flow from it, but repentance itself is the turn — a new mind that walks a new
> way.

**d1-l1-en-christo** · card 28
The phrase *en Christō* ("in Christ"), which recurs over 160 times in Paul,
points to:
- **✓ the believer's real inclusion in Christ, so His death and life count as ours**
- imitating Christ's example closely
- living inside a Christian community
- holding correct beliefs about Christ

> To be "in Christ" is to be joined to Him so completely that what is true of Him
> becomes true of you (Gal 2:20) — crucified, raised, and seated with Him.

**d1-l1-huiothesia** · card 29
*Huiothesia*, the word for adoption, literally means:
- **✓ placing as a son**
- raising an orphan
- naming an heir
- taking in a guest

> *Huios* ("son") + *thesis* ("placing"). In the Roman world it was an
> irrevocable legal act granting the full rights of a natural-born heir — Paul's
> picture of our standing in God's family (Rom 8:15).

**d1-l1-basileia** · card 32
*Basileia tou Theou* ("kingdom of God") refers first of all to:
- **✓ God's reign and rule breaking in**
- a place believers go after death
- the visible church on earth
- the nation of Israel restored

> *Basileia* is "kingship" before it is "kingdom" — the active rule of God,
> arriving in Jesus' ministry (Matt 4:17) and not yet complete. A realm follows
> from a reign.

### Holy Scripture

**d1-l1-theopneustos** · card 33
*Theopneustos*, Paul's word for Scripture in 2 Timothy 3:16, means:
- **✓ God-breathed**
- God-spoken
- God-given
- God-approved

> *Theos* + *pneō*, "to breathe." Scripture is breathed out by God — divine in
> origin, even as real human authors wrote it.

**d1-l1-logos** · card 35
When John calls Jesus the *Logos* ("the Word") in John 1, he means:
- **✓ God's own self-expression, personally present**
- a written record of God's laws
- a wise saying or piece of teaching
- a prophetic message spoken aloud

> *Logos* is speech that reveals the speaker. John says the Word who was with God
> and was God became flesh — God making Himself known in person. A spoken
> prophetic message is *rhēma*.

### Church & Sacraments

**d1-l1-ekklesia** · card 36
*Ekklēsia*, the New Testament word for "church," means:
- **✓ an assembly of those called out and gathered**
- a sacred building set apart for worship
- a ranked order of ordained leaders
- a region under a bishop's care

> *Ek* ("out") + *kaleō* ("call") — people summoned together by God, not a
> building or an institution. The English "church" descends from a different word
> (*kyriakon*, "belonging to the Lord").

**d1-l1-baptisma** · card 38
*Baptisma* / *baptizō*, the word behind "baptism," basically means:
- **✓ to dip or immerse**
- to sprinkle lightly
- to wash the hands
- to anoint with oil

> The same word was used for dyeing cloth and for a ship going under. Paul reads
> the going-down and coming-up as dying and rising with Christ (Rom 6:3–4).

**d1-l1-koinonia** · card 39
*Koinōnia*, Paul's word for the Lord's Supper in 1 Corinthians 10:16, carries the
sense of:
- **✓ a sharing / participation in**
- a remembrance of
- a thanksgiving for
- a covenant renewal with

> "The cup of blessing... is it not a *koinōnia* in the blood of Christ?" The
> table is real participation in Christ, more than a memorial. *Anamnēsis* is
> "remembrance," *eucharistia* "thanksgiving" — adjacent words, different weight.

### Christian Life

**d1-l1-agape** · card 41
In the New Testament, *agapē* is set apart from the other Greek words for love
because it names:
- **✓ a deliberate, self-giving love that seeks another's good**
- love between close friends
- romantic or desiring love
- natural affection within a family

> *Philia* is friendship, *erōs* desire, *storgē* family affection. *Agapē* is
> love as a chosen commitment — the love God is, and the love He commands
> (John 13:34–35).

**d1-l1-hypomone** · card 43
*Hypomonē*, translated "perseverance" or "endurance," literally means:
- **✓ remaining under**
- pressing forward
- standing firm against
- waiting quietly for

> *Hypo* ("under") + *menō* ("remain"). Not gritting through or escaping the
> weight, but staying under it until it finishes its work (Heb 10:36; Jas 1:3).

### Last Things

**d1-l1-parousia** · card 46
*Parousia*, the main New Testament word for Christ's return, means:
- **✓ arrival and personal presence**
- the unveiling of what was hidden
- the day of the Lord's wrath
- a sudden snatching away

> *Parousia* was used for the state visit of a king. Two other words name facets
> of the same event: *apokalypsis* ("unveiling") and *epiphaneia* ("appearing").

**d1-l1-zoe-aionios** · card 50
*Zōē aiōnios* ("eternal life"), as Jesus defines it in John 17:3, is primarily:
- **✓ a kind of life — knowing God — that begins now**
- unending existence after death
- a reward handed out at the final judgment
- the soul's natural immortality

> *Aiōnios* means "of the age to come" as much as "endless." Jesus locates
> eternal life in *knowing* the Father and the Son — a quality of life already
> begun, not only a length of time still ahead.

## L2 — Scripture & History (35)

Where a doctrine is anchored, what the passage claims, and the council, creed,
heresy, or figure behind it. Distractors are real references / real councils /
real heresies / real figures / true-sounding but wrong doctrinal statements.

### Trinity & Nature of God

**d1-l2-homoousios-arianism** · card 1
The word *homoousios* was written into the Nicene Creed (325) to rule out:
- **✓ Arianism**
- Pelagianism
- Nestorianism
- Gnosticism

> Arius taught the Son was the first and highest *creature*. *Homoousios* — same
> being as the Father — says the Son is fully God and uncreated. (Pelagianism is
> about sin and grace; Nestorianism about Christ's natures; Gnosticism about
> matter and secret knowledge.)

**d1-l2-begotten-not-made** · card 3
In the Nicene Creed, the phrase "begotten, not made" means that the Son:
- **✓ is eternally from the Father's own being, never brought into existence**
- was the first thing the Father made, before anything else
- proceeds from the Father in the same way the Spirit does
- became the Son at the moment of the incarnation

> "Begotten" keeps the Son's origin *in* God and outside of time; "not made"
> denies He is a creature. The creed's point against Arius: there was never a
> time when the Son was not.

**d1-l2-spirit-constantinople** · card 4
The Council of Constantinople (381) expanded the creed to confess that the Holy
Spirit:
- **✓ is worshiped and glorified together with the Father and the Son**
- proceeds from the Son alone
- is a created power rather than a person
- became divine at Pentecost

> Constantinople settled the Spirit's full deity against those who honored the
> Father and Son but treated the Spirit as a force. (The "from the Son"
> question — the *filioque* — is a later Western addition and a separate dispute.)

**d1-l2-creator-gnosticism** · card 6
The church confessed the one true God as "maker of heaven and earth, of all
things visible and invisible" chiefly against:
- **✓ Gnosticism, which said matter is evil and made by a lesser god**
- Arianism, which said the Son is a creature
- Docetism, which said Christ only seemed to have a body
- Montanism, which claimed fresh prophecy above Scripture

> Gnostic systems split a good spirit-world from an evil material one made by a
> lower deity. Genesis 1 and the creed insist the one God made everything, and
> made it good.

**d1-l2-perichoresis-damascus** · card 5
The term *perichōrēsis* — the divine Persons containing one another without
blurring — was given its classic form by:
- **✓ John of Damascus (8th century)**
- Augustine of Hippo
- Thomas Aquinas
- Athanasius of Alexandria

> John of Damascus used it to guard the Trinity from two errors at once:
> dividing God into three, or collapsing Him into one.

### Jesus Christ

**d1-l2-resurrection-futile** · card 14
Paul writes that if one thing is not true, "your faith is worthless; you are
still in your sins" (1 Corinthians 15:17). He means:
- **✓ the resurrection**
- the incarnation
- the atoning death
- the second coming

> 1 Corinthians 15 stakes the whole gospel on the risen Christ. Take the
> resurrection away and, Paul says, forgiveness itself falls with it.

**d1-l2-chalcedon-eutyches** · card 11
Chalcedon (451) confessed Christ in "two natures, without confusion, without
change, without division, without separation." It condemned Nestorius, who split
Christ into two persons — and also:
- **✓ Eutyches, who blended the two natures into one**
- Arius, who denied the Son's deity
- the Docetists, who denied Christ had a real body
- Sabellius, who denied the three Persons

> The four "withouts" guard both flanks at once: the natures are neither divided
> into two persons nor fused into a third kind of thing.

**d1-l2-chalcedon-defined** · card 9
The Council of Chalcedon (451) is the council that formally defined:
- **✓ how the divine and human natures relate in the one person of Christ**
- the doctrine of the Trinity
- which books belong in the New Testament
- that Christ has both a divine and a human will

> Chalcedon's subject was the person of Christ. The Trinity was settled at
> Nicaea/Constantinople; the two-wills question at Constantinople III (681); the
> canon was never fixed by a single council decree.

**d1-l2-yom-kippur** · card 13
On the Day of Atonement, the act the high priest performed once a year — which
Hebrews says Christ fulfilled once for all — was:
- **✓ entering the Holy of Holies with sacrificial blood**
- breaking bread with the people
- reading the covenant aloud to the nation
- lighting the seven-branched lampstand

> Hebrews 9:12: Christ "entered the holy place once for all... by His own blood,"
> the true and final Day of Atonement.

**d1-l2-firstfruits** · card 14
Paul calls the risen Christ "the firstfruits of those who are asleep" (1 Cor
15:20) to make the point that:
- **✓ His resurrection is the beginning and guarantee of ours**
- He was the first person ever restored to life
- only the apostles will share in resurrection
- resurrection is a picture, not a bodily event

> Firstfruits are the first of a harvest that is sure to follow. Christ raised is
> the pledge that His people will be raised too.

**d1-l2-right-hand** · card 15
The Nicene Creed confesses that Christ "ascended into heaven and is seated at the
right hand of the Father." Being "seated at the right hand" signifies:
- **✓ active rule and authority over all things**
- rest from His work, with rule handed back to the Father
- a fixed location He waits in until He returns
- an equality with the Father that He had given up on earth

> The right hand is the place of power. The ascension is Christ's enthronement —
> our own human nature, in His person, now reigns (Eph 4:10).

**d1-l2-virgin-birth-creeds** · card 10
The virgin birth, confessed in both the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds, safeguards
two truths about Jesus at once: His true humanity (born of Mary) and:
- **✓ His divine origin — conceived by the Spirit, not by human will**
- His sinlessness, which could be secured no other way
- His legal descent from David, traced through Joseph
- His existence before the world was made

> Born of Mary, He is genuinely one of us; conceived by the Spirit, His coming is
> God's own act, not a human achievement. (Scripture never makes the virgin birth
> the *mechanism* of His sinlessness.)

### Holy Spirit

**d1-l2-nicodemus** · card 17
Jesus told Nicodemus that no one can see the kingdom of God without:
- **✓ being born again, from above, by the Spirit**
- being circumcised as a son of Abraham
- keeping the law of Moses without fault
- being baptized by John in the Jordan

> John 3. Entrance into the kingdom is not reform or pedigree but a new birth
> that only the Spirit can give.

**d1-l2-glory-to-glory** · card 18
"We all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being
transformed... from glory to glory" (2 Corinthians 3:18) is the anchor verse for:
- **✓ sanctification**
- regeneration
- justification
- glorification

> Regeneration is the new birth, justification the verdict, glorification the
> future state. This verse — transformation "from glory to glory" — is the
> present, ongoing work: sanctification.

**d1-l2-imputed-righteousness** · card 27
The Reformers taught that in justification God credits the believer with:
- **✓ the righteousness of Christ, reckoned to their account**
- a righteousness of their own that faith gradually produces
- the surplus merits of the saints
- a clean slate — the past wiped, with nothing put in its place

> "God credits Christ's perfect obedience to our account." We are counted
> righteous not because we have become so, but because Christ's record is
> reckoned as ours.

### Salvation & Gospel

**d1-l2-augustine-pelagius** · card 22
Augustine developed and defended the doctrine of original sin against:
- **✓ Pelagius**
- Arius
- Nestorius
- Marcion

> Pelagius taught that people can choose good without grace. Augustine held that
> Adam's fall left all humanity needing grace even to *will* the good.

**d1-l2-romans4-justification** · card 27
"To the one who does not work but believes in Him who justifies the ungodly, his
faith is credited as righteousness" (Romans 4:5) is the anchor verse for:
- **✓ justification**
- faith
- grace
- repentance

> Paul's sharpest statement that righteousness is *credited*, not earned — the
> verse Luther built the Reformation recovery of justification on.

**d1-l2-gospel-first-importance** · card 23
Paul says the message he "received" and "passed on... as of first importance"
(1 Corinthians 15:3–4) is that:
- **✓ Christ died for our sins, was buried, and was raised on the third day**
- we are to love God with all our heart and our neighbor as ourselves
- the kingdom of God has come near
- the Spirit is poured out on all who ask

> Each other option is a true part of Jesus' message, but Paul names the
> death-burial-resurrection as the core content of the gospel itself.

**d1-l2-faith-empty-hand** · card 24
"By grace you have been saved through faith... not as a result of works, so that
no one may boast" (Ephesians 2:8–9). In this verse, faith is:
- **✓ the empty hand that receives the gift**
- the work that earns the reward
- a level of certainty God requires
- the merit God accepts in place of obedience

> Faith saves not because it is a great achievement but because of what it lays
> hold of. The moment faith becomes something we're credited *for*, grace stops
> being grace.

**d1-l2-luther-reformation** · card 27
Which discovery of Martin Luther's touched off the Protestant Reformation?
- **✓ that we are made right with God through faith in Christ, not by our own works**
- that church services should be held in the local language, not Latin
- that the Bible should be printed cheaply for ordinary people to read
- that priests should be free to marry

> The other three were real changes the Reformation brought, but they followed
> from the discovery at its center: reading Romans, Luther saw that God's
> righteousness is a gift received by faith, not a standard he had to reach.

**d1-l2-justification-meaning** · card 27
Which statement best states what *justification* means?
- **✓ God legally declares the sinner righteous in Christ**
- God gradually makes the sinner holy over a lifetime
- God overlooks the sin for Christ's sake
- God rewards persevering faith with righteousness

> Option 2 is sanctification. Option 3 misses that Christ's righteousness is
> actually credited to us. Option 4 turns faith into a merit. Justification is a
> verdict, declared once and complete.

**d1-l2-abba-adoption** · card 29
"You have received a spirit of adoption as sons, by which we cry out, 'Abba!
Father!'" (Romans 8:15) anchors the doctrine of:
- **✓ adoption**
- regeneration
- justification
- assurance

> Regeneration is the new birth, justification the verdict, assurance the
> confidence that follows. This verse names the new *family standing* — adoption.

**d1-l2-calvin-hinge** · card 28
John Calvin called union with Christ "the hinge of salvation," meaning that:
- **✓ apart from being joined to Christ, everything He accomplished stays useless to us**
- it is the one doctrine on which the Reformers could not agree
- it replaces the need for personal faith
- it is a further blessing added some time after justification

> Calvin's point: the cross and resurrection only become *ours* because the
> Spirit unites us to the One who died and rose.

**d1-l2-jeremiah-new-covenant** · card 30
The new covenant that Jesus sealed in His blood was promised centuries earlier
by:
- **✓ Jeremiah (31:31–34), quoted in full in Hebrews 8**
- Moses, at the second giving of the law
- David, in the royal psalms
- Malachi, in the last book of the Old Testament

> Jeremiah's promise — the law written on the heart, sins remembered no more — is
> the longest Old Testament quotation anywhere in the New.

### Holy Scripture

**d1-l2-theopneustos-verse** · card 33
The Greek word *theopneustos* ("God-breathed") appears in:
- **✓ 2 Timothy 3:16**
- John 1:1
- 2 Peter 1:21
- Hebrews 4:12

> Each reference is a real text about God's word or the Word — but "All Scripture
> is *theopneustos*" is Paul to Timothy. (2 Peter 1:21 is close in theme: "men
> moved by the Holy Spirit spoke from God.")

**d1-l2-sola-scriptura** · card 34
The Reformation principle *sola scriptura* means that:
- **✓ Scripture is the final authority, above tradition and church decree**
- Scripture is the only book a Christian may read
- the Bible interprets itself with no need of teachers
- only the recorded words of Jesus carry authority

> *Sola scriptura* doesn't deny the value of tradition, teachers, or creeds — it
> puts them all under the higher authority of God's written word.

### Church & Sacraments

**d1-l2-baptism-romans6** · card 38
Paul explains the meaning of baptism in Romans 6 as:
- **✓ being buried and raised with Christ**
- a washing that removes the guilt of daily sins
- a pledge to keep the moral law
- a rite that completes what faith leaves unfinished

> "Buried with Him through baptism into death... so we too might walk in newness
> of life." The going-under and coming-up enact dying and rising with Christ.

**d1-l2-koinonia-sharing** · card 39
Paul's word for what happens at the Lord's table, *koinōnia*, means the meal is:
- **✓ a real sharing in the body and blood of Christ**
- only a mental exercise of remembering a past event
- a fresh sacrifice of Christ offered each time
- a private moment between the believer and God alone

> "The cup... is it not a *koinōnia* in the blood of Christ?" More than a
> memorial, less than a re-sacrifice — genuine participation in Christ, and in
> one another as one body.

**d1-l2-body-organism** · card 37
When Paul calls the church "the body of Christ," his point is that believers
are:
- **✓ one living body, mutually dependent, with Christ as the head**
- a well-run organization with Christ as its founder
- separate branches that do not need one another
- a building in which Christ is only the cornerstone

> The metaphor is an organism, not an institution — animated by Christ, so that
> "if one member suffers, all suffer together" (1 Cor 12).

### Christian Life

**d1-l2-martyria** · card 45
The Greek word *martyria* ("witness") came, over time, also to mean "martyrdom"
because:
- **✓ many early Christians sealed their testimony with their death**
- witnessing could only be done in a court of law
- the word originally meant "to suffer"
- only those who had been martyred were permitted to evangelize

> To witness to Christ under Rome could cost your life. The word for "testimony"
> absorbed the price often paid to give it.

**d1-l2-mathetes-rabbi** · card 40
In the first century, being someone's *mathētēs* ("disciple") meant:
- **✓ following a teacher everywhere, learning by watching and imitating**
- enrolling in a school for a course of study
- making a one-time profession of belief
- supporting a teacher with money

> A disciple shared the rabbi's whole life. Jesus calls for the same — not
> agreement at a distance but a life spent following.

### Last Things

**d1-l2-maranatha** · card 46
The earliest recorded Christian prayer, *Maranatha* ("Come, Lord!"), expressed
the church's:
- **✓ longing for Christ's return**
- gratitude for the resurrection
- plea for the Spirit to be poured out
- sorrow under persecution

> 1 Corinthians 16:22. From the first, the church lived leaning toward the
> *parousia* — "Come, Lord Jesus."

**d1-l2-greek-immortality** · card 47
Confessing the bodily resurrection set early Christianity apart from Greek
philosophy, which taught instead:
- **✓ the immortality of the soul, freed from the body**
- the extinction of the person at death
- endless cycles of reincarnation
- the sleep of the soul until judgment

> Greek thought treated the body as a prison to escape. Christians confess the
> *body* raised — "all who are in the tombs will hear His voice" (John 5:28).

**d1-l2-judgment-seat-believer** · card 48
"We must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ" (2 Corinthians 5:10).
For those who trust Christ, this judgment means:
- **✓ they are declared righteous by grace and openly vindicated**
- their salvation is still in the balance until the verdict is read
- they must answer for every sin as if it had never been forgiven
- they are excused from appearing at all

> Judgment holds no condemnation for those in Christ (Rom 8:1). It is where
> God's justice is seen to be done and His people are publicly cleared.

**d1-l2-new-earth-renewal** · card 49
The biblical hope of "a new heaven and a new earth" is best described as:
- **✓ the renewal of creation, with God coming down to dwell with His people**
- the destruction of the material world so souls can live in heaven
- a return to the garden of Eden exactly as it first was
- a poetic image with no bearing on the physical world

> Revelation 21: the first earth "passed away," but the vision is God descending
> to dwell *here* — creation liberated, not discarded.

## L3 — Connections (25)

How the doctrines fit together — the redemptive arc, cross-card logic, and
distinguishing doctrines people run together. The hardest tier. Every distractor
is a real doctrine or a real position, never a card title.

### Trinity & Nature of God

**d1-l3-trinity-drawn-in** · card 5
The doctrine of the Trinity matters for salvation because:
- **✓ we are drawn into the love the Father, Son, and Spirit have always shared**
- it is a puzzle designed to test whether our faith is sincere
- it shows God changed over time, from Father to Son to Spirit
- it proves God is finally beyond human understanding

> The "divine dance" is not a riddle to solve. Salvation is being included, in
> Christ and by the Spirit, in the communion that God has always been.

**d1-l3-imago-dei-dignity** · card 7
That every person bears God's image is the foundation for:
- **✓ human dignity — and the call to see Christ in the least of these**
- the belief that people are basically good by nature
- the idea that every religion is equally true
- the claim that humans are only unusually clever animals

> The image is defaced by sin but never erased. It grounds why every human life
> has worth, regardless of usefulness, status, or condition.

### Jesus Christ

**d1-l3-why-god-and-man** · card 11
Why must the Savior be *both* fully God and fully human?
- **✓ as man He can stand in our place; as God His work has infinite worth**
- so that He could feel both joy and sorrow like us
- so that an old prophecy about a God-man would come true
- so that His teaching would carry more authority

> A substitute must belong to those he represents — so, human. But no mere human
> could bear the weight of the world's sin and win — so, God. Both, or no gospel.

**d1-l3-atonement-pictures** · card 13
The New Testament describes what the cross achieved through several pictures.
Which one centers on a broken *relationship* restored — enemies made friends, the
far-off brought near?
- **✓ reconciliation**
- ransom — a price paid to free a captive
- propitiation — God's wrath turned away
- redemption — being bought back out of slavery

> All four are real biblical images of the one cross. "God was in Christ
> *reconciling* the world to Himself" (2 Cor 5:19) is the relational one: the
> enmity is ended, and we are brought home.

**d1-l3-resurrection-guarantees-ours** · card 14
Christ's resurrection is not only His own vindication. It is also:
- **✓ the guarantee and pattern of the believer's resurrection**
- a private miracle with no wider consequence
- proof that the human soul is naturally immortal
- the moment He first became the Son of God

> "The firstfruits of those who are asleep" (1 Cor 15:20). Because He was raised
> bodily, His people will be too — same event, same kind of life.

**d1-l3-ascension-humanity-reigns** · card 15
The ascension means that, right now:
- **✓ a glorified human being rules at the center of all things**
- Christ is absent and inactive until He returns
- His human nature was left behind on earth
- the Spirit stepped in as a lesser replacement

> Our own nature, in the person of the Son, is enthroned at the Father's right
> hand. Where the Head has gone, the body will follow.

### Holy Spirit

**d1-l3-regeneration-begins-life** · card 17
Regeneration is the new birth by the Spirit. Its place in the Christian life is
that it:
- **✓ begins it — the start of new life, not a later goal to reach**
- completes it — nothing of importance follows
- is needed by some believers but not others
- is simply another name for sanctification

> You are born again once; you grow for a lifetime. Regeneration is the door,
> sanctification the road.

**d1-l3-indwelling-body-temple** · card 21
Because the Spirit indwells every believer, the New Testament calls the body:
- **✓ a temple — God's dwelling, to be honored**
- a prison the soul must escape
- irrelevant, since only the soul matters to God
- neutral, neither good nor bad in itself

> "You are a temple of God and the Spirit of God dwells in you" (1 Cor 3:16).
> What we do with the body is not spiritually indifferent.

### Salvation & Gospel

**d1-l3-fall-makes-grace-necessary** · card 22
Which doctrine describes the condition that makes *grace* necessary?
- **✓ sin and the fall**
- repentance
- suffering
- discipleship

> Grace is unearned rescue; it only means anything against a fallen, helpless
> condition. "Where sin increased, grace abounded all the more" (Romans 5:20).

**d1-l3-sin-is-condition** · card 22
Scripture treats sin as more than wrong actions. It is also:
- **✓ a broken condition we are born into — needing rescue, not just correction**
- a bad habit that enough willpower can break
- an illusion that fades once a person knows better
- a problem that only affects some kinds of people

> "We don't just commit sins; we are sinners who need rescue." This is why the
> answer is a new birth and a Savior, not merely better behavior.

**d1-l3-union-is-the-ground** · card 28
Justification, adoption, sanctification, and glorification all come to us by way
of:
- **✓ union with Christ**
- repeated acts of faith that keep them active
- the sacraments administered by the church
- a gradual growth in personal holiness

> None of Christ's benefits reach us in isolation. They are ours only because the
> Spirit joins us to Christ Himself — everything else follows from that.

**d1-l3-faith-that-works** · cards 24 & 25
How do faith and works relate in salvation?
- **✓ we are saved by faith alone, but faith that is alive bears the fruit of works**
- works supply part of the merit and faith supplies the rest
- works come first, and faith completes what they begin
- works have no place in the Christian life at all

> Paul: saved by grace through faith, not works. James: faith without works is
> dead. Not a contradiction — a living faith always shows.

**d1-l3-repentance-and-faith** · cards 25 & 26
Repentance and faith in conversion are:
- **✓ one turning seen from two sides — from sin, and to Christ**
- two separate stages, often years apart
- repentance for unbelievers, faith for believers
- alternatives, where either one is enough

> You cannot turn *to* Christ without turning *from* what you trusted before.
> They are the front and back of a single step.

**d1-l3-kingdom-already-not-yet** · card 32
When Jesus announced "the kingdom of God is at hand," the New Testament goes on
to picture the kingdom as:
- **✓ already begun in Christ, and not yet complete**
- entirely future, arriving only when He returns
- entirely present, fully here in its final form now
- only an inward feeling, never anything outward

> Christ reigns now in His people; the kingdom comes in fullness at His return.
> The church lives in the overlap of the ages.

**d1-l3-new-covenant-power** · card 30
The new covenant differs from the old chiefly in that:
- **✓ the Spirit writes God's law on the heart and gives power to keep it**
- God no longer cares how His people live
- it applies only to the nation of Israel
- it removes the need for forgiveness of sins

> Jeremiah's promise: not new rules, but a new heart and an indwelling Spirit —
> the power the law could command but never supply.

**d1-l3-justification-to-adoption** · cards 27 & 29
Justification *declares* us righteous. Which doctrine names the new *relationship*
we are brought into?
- **✓ adoption**
- regeneration
- sanctification
- salvation

> Justification is the courtroom — the verdict "not guilty." Adoption is the
> family room — "my child." Romans 8:15: the Spirit of adoption by which we cry
> "Abba, Father."

**d1-l3-justified-vs-sanctified** · cards 27 & 18
A believer says, "I was justified the day I trusted Christ, and I've been
sanctified a little more every day since." What distinction is she making
correctly?
- **✓ a finished verdict vs. an ongoing work**
- a legal change vs. a relational one
- God's part vs. our part
- the Father's work vs. the Spirit's

> Justification is once-for-all and complete; sanctification is lifelong and
> gradual. Both are grace. Collapsing the two produces either pride or despair.

**d1-l3-regeneration-vs-adoption** · cards 17 & 29
Regeneration and adoption both happen at conversion, but they answer different
questions. Regeneration answers "what changed inside me?" Adoption answers:
- **✓ "whose family am I in?"**
- "am I really forgiven?"
- "will I make it to the end?"
- "what do I have to do now?"

> Regeneration = a new nature, born of the Spirit (Titus 3:5). Adoption = a new
> status, a permanent place in God's household.

### Holy Scripture

**d1-l3-ot-points-to-christ** · card 35
To read the Old Testament as a Christian is to:
- **✓ look for how it prepares for and points forward to Christ**
- treat it as no longer relevant to believers
- read it only for timeless moral rules
- assume it speaks of a different God than the New Testament

> The risen Jesus, "beginning with Moses and all the Prophets," showed the
> disciples how all Scripture spoke of Him (Luke 24:27).

### Church & Sacraments

**d1-l3-church-local-and-universal** · card 36
The New Testament speaks of the church as *both*:
- **✓ every believer everywhere, and the particular congregation you gather with**
- a building, and the organization that runs it
- the clergy, and separately the laity
- Israel, and separately the nations

> One church, seen two ways: the universal body of all the redeemed, and the
> local assembly where that body takes visible shape.

**d1-l3-sacraments-visible-signs** · cards 38 & 39
Baptism and the Lord's Supper are set apart from other practices because they:
- **✓ are visible signs Christ Himself gave of an invisible grace**
- earn grace for the person who performs them correctly
- are symbols with no real link to what they point to
- take the place of personal faith in Christ

> Water, bread, and cup are given by Christ to show and seal what the gospel
> promises — dying and rising with Him, sharing in His body and blood.

### Christian Life

**d1-l3-suffering-forms-christ** · card 44
The New Testament does not promise escape from suffering. Instead it says God
uses trials to:
- **✓ refine faith and form the character of Christ in us**
- punish believers for sins they have hidden
- earn extra reward for those strong enough to bear it
- expose which people were never really Christians

> "The testing of your faith produces endurance" (Jas 1:3). We are not promised a
> way around suffering but God's presence within it, working good.

**d1-l3-perseverance-preserved** · card 43
Perseverance does not mean a believer never struggles or doubts. It means:
- **✓ God is faithful to finish His work, and calls His people to endure to the end**
- a Christian may live any way at all and remain secure
- salvation must be re-earned through each new day
- any season of doubt proves that salvation was never real

> Two things held together: "He who began a good work in you will complete it"
> (Phil 1:6), and "you have need of endurance" (Heb 10:36). Faith may grow weak,
> but it is meant to press on, kept by God.

### Last Things

**d1-l3-blessed-hope-motivates** · card 46
The "blessed hope" of Christ's return is meant to produce:
- **✓ watchful, holy living and faithful service now**
- date-setting and withdrawal from ordinary life
- mainly fear of being caught unprepared
- indifference, since no one knows the timing

> Because He is coming, and could come at any time, the New Testament says: stay
> awake, keep working, live clean.

**d1-l3-body-and-earth-together** · cards 47 & 49
The resurrection of the body and the new earth belong together because:
- **✓ God redeems the whole creation — embodied people for a renewed world**
- both are only word-pictures for a non-physical heaven
- the body is raised, but the earth is finally destroyed
- the earth is renewed, but people remain disembodied spirits

> The Christian hope is not rescue *from* the material world but its renewal —
> raised people at home on a healed earth, with God dwelling among them.
