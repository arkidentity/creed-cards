Reviewed 2026-10-27 against `lib/goDeeper.ts` (uncommitted) and `lib/cardData.ts`. ## 3. Polish (consistency, jargon, light precision)

- **Irenaeus pronunciation** — #12 has `ih-ruh-NAY-us`; #6, #7 (live) have `eye-ruh-NAY-us`. Match the live one.
- **#38 Baptism** wordNote: Mark 7:4 uses the noun baptismos for washing cups (the verb is for washing themselves). "Mark 7:4 uses a related word for washing cups and pots."
- **#34 Authority**: "Jesus declared all foods clean (Mark 7:19)" is Mark's own comment and is translation-dependent (KJV reads differently). "Mark notes that Jesus' teaching made all foods clean."
- **#26 Repentance**: "Luther **posted** 95 statements" — whether he nailed them up is debated; "posted" is fine, or "published". Low priority.
- **#39 Lord's Supper**: "at a Passover supper" follows Matthew/Mark/Luke; John's timing differs. Fine as is; optional "at the Passover meal, as Matthew, Mark, and Luke tell it".
- **#10**: parthenos "had never been with a man" — usage was looser (Genesis 34:3 LXX uses it of Dinah). Optional: "the usual Greek word for a young unmarried woman, normally meaning one who had never been with a man."
- **Jargon a new believer may trip on**: "God-fearers" (#42 wordNote, unexplained), "Hall of Faith" (#25), "the Reformation" (#26, #27 people lines), "intercession" (#15, inside a quote — fine), "deacon" (#1, live). Suggest: #42 "Jews and non-Jews who worshiped Israel's God"; #25 drop "sometimes called the Hall of Faith".
- **#21 Indwelling**: Romans 8:11 actually uses the verb enoikeō (the root of enoikēsis). `usesWord: false` is defensible (noun isn't used), but it's worth one word in the note: "uses the verb."
- **#45**: Acts 22:20 ("Stephen Your witness") uses martys — a stronger verse than Acts 1:8 if you want one flagged `usesWord: true`. Optional.
- **#16**: "In older English, to comfort someone meant to strengthen them" — accurate (Latin confortare). Keep.

## usesWord audit

All flags check out, with a strict rule of "true = that verse uses the card's exact word (noun vs. verb counted as different)". Cards that count a verb form as false (#25 John 3:16, #26 Mark 1:15, #38 Acts 2:38, #41 Matthew 5:44, #45 Acts 1:8) are consistent with that rule. One inconsistency: #8 Psalm 115:3 and #12 Romans 3:25 are fine; #13 Leviticus 16:30/17:11 are marked true for the verb kipper while the card word is the noun kippur — slightly looser than the rule used elsewhere. Either flip to false or accept "same root" for Hebrew.
